# Stage 4 — Rewrite and review the complete latest batch

Read `00-shared-context.md` and the completed stages 1–3. This is the content acceptance
test for the system, not an excuse to hand-edit sixteen files while leaving generation broken.

## Goal

All 16 latest episodes should work for a curious nonspecialist listening once. Preserve
supported science, useful host personality, source attribution, and the improved titles
unless a title promises more than the final episode delivers.

## Inputs and evidence discipline

Read current paired artifacts and their original selected evidence. Recover evidence from
the existing ignored local database or the documented pipeline where available. Do not
substitute a press release, an earlier draft, or your own subject knowledge for the paper
packet. If source access is missing, state the blocker rather than inventing explanations.
Never add that database, credentials or full raw source texts to git.

Inspect whether each paper fits its show. In particular, assess the eye-mechanics paper
for Marginal Gains and NMR paper for Star Stuff. If the match is poor, use the established
selection/ingestion path to obtain a genuinely relevant candidate within the task's budget,
or flag the episode as withheld. Do not fix off-topic science with a sports/stars metaphor.
Avoid changing good source selections unnecessarily.

## Writing target

Aim for a short episode that clearly answers one question. Roughly 350–500 spoken words is
a working target, not permission to pad thin evidence or discard necessary uncertainty.
Respect configured structural limits unless deliberately changing and testing that contract.

Keep what a listener needs:
- A concrete, evidence-supported opening and a title worth tapping.
- One prerequisite idea explained before specialist terminology.
- What researchers did as ordinary actions: compared pictures, weighed hives, followed
  people over time, or trained a model on simulated data.
- The main finding stated plainly and connected to its meaning.
- One concise limitation, with all material boundaries preserved.
- Correct author credit, source metadata, chosen spoken-citation policy and exact sign-offs.

Cut what chiefly demonstrates expertise: instrument/model identifiers, gene names, exhaustive
category lists, secondary statistics, abstract statistical terminology and unrelated host
asides. Keep essential study design/population facts even when they complicate the story.
Do not replace accurate terms with misleading analogies or fabricate everyday examples as
observed events.

Fix the known defects specifically: internal packet language, “Comparison:”/“Limitations:”
labels, repeated paper titles, repeated caveats, “the paper, with the same title” after
retitling, and awkward punctuation such as a question mark followed by a full stop inside
an episode-title quotation.

For dialogue, make each exchange answer a real question. Preserve the two/four named hosts,
stable IDs, distinct voices, contract participation limits and correct individual sign-offs.

## Work in a bounded sequence

1. Pilot the repaired production generation/review path on a difficult co-hosted episode
   and a difficult solo. Read the entire result. If it still resembles a narrated abstract,
   repair the system before spending calls on the rest.
2. Process the remaining batch within existing call caps. Do not raise caps silently.
3. For every changed script, run structure, exact source-quote and factual checks, then
   audience review. Repair substantive failures; do not repeatedly rescore unchanged text.
4. Read every final script yourself. Confirm that a novice can reconstruct the finding and
   its limitation without needing the paper. Avoid claiming real user testing occurred.
5. Synchronize JSON, Markdown, manifest titles/word counts/statuses and any operator state
   needed to avoid the next export restoring old text. Keep superseded evidence/reviews
   traceable through existing history rather than adding duplicate public artifacts.
6. Ensure every approval fingerprint matches the final script. No polishing after approval
   without invalidating and rerunning affected checks.

## Acceptance and handoff

All 16 entries must have honest individual dispositions: approved, withheld, or blocked.
Do not describe 14 approvals as a complete 16-show release. If any cannot be approved, keep
the live feed unchanged and report the exact content/evidence/budget blocker.

For an approved full batch, report a compact table of show, final title, factual decision,
audience decision and material remaining caveat. A few before/after excerpts should show
that the difficult explanations improved; do not paste all sixteen scripts into the final
response. Keep report data in the existing manifest/review system where practical.

Done when artifacts and states agree, each final script has been read and reviewed, all
required tests pass, no internal instructions remain in speech, and the batch is ready for
stage 5. Do not claim it is live before rendering and public verification complete.
